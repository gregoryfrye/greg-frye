Four products went to market in New York before anyone else's: Dimes, Ounces, Moonwalkers, and Hash Coins. The roadmap was built to find those gaps.

**Flower** — Dimes (first to market), Eighths, Quarters, Ounces (first to market), Grounds, Kief-Infused Grounds, Moon Rocks

**Prerolls** — Joints, 7-Packs, Moonwalkers (first to market), Hash-Infused Packs

**Concentrates** — Hash Coins (first to market, #1 best-seller), Cured Rosin, RSO, Solventless Diamonds

**Vapes** — Carts, Rosin-Infused, Liquid Diamond

Ounces launched when the business needed working capital — a volume SKU to bridge cashflow. Not a creative decision, but the creative team was where the call got made.

I ran naming across products, strains, and blends. Genetics we inherited kept their original names and we printed them on the package — the same transparency a produce label gives you when it names the varietal. Blend names I developed against three constraints at once: what consumers were asking for, what the harvest could actually supply, and what the brand could credibly say.

**The closed loop.** Whole-plant thinking started as an extension of the farm's regenerative practice — the same logic that governs soil applied to material. Craft beer, single-origin coffee, natural wine had all built categories on process transparency and graded output. Cannabis hadn't.

It became literal once I understood the full process from seed to finished product. Solventless static sift hash was made from the byproduct of our own flower: high-margin product from material most operators discount. Every grade of the harvest had a destination.

The clearest version came from teaching budtenders at the farm. Papa Smurf hash arrived three to six months after Papa Smurf flower — same strain, same field, later in the process, because processing and curing take the time they take. People rallied around that. And the flower had already earned enough trust that they believed in the hash before they tried it.

Committing to solventless — common in Europe, still rare in the US — meant the entire concentrate line was categorically differentiated, straight through to pure solventless THCA diamonds. It also meant the products carried the values on their own. Someone with no time for the farm story still got a differentiated product in their hand.

**RSO.** The category was sold in syringes — a format that reads clinical and asks a first-time buyer to dose an unfamiliar full-spectrum concentrate out of an unmarked plunger. Wrong for our customer, wrong for a brand built to sit next to produce.

We became the first brand to bring a newly patented dispenser to market instead. It made RSO measurable, portable, and approachable, and opened the product to people the syringe had been turning away. Once it proved out, we negotiated exclusivity on the dispenser — protecting a ready-to-use RSO category we had effectively created.
