A wordmark built to sit closer to a farm than a dispensary. Wide-tracked and unadorned, paired with a high-contrast display serif that carried the campaign voice. The system had to hold two registers at once — the credibility of the growing operation and the wit of the consumer work — without either undercutting the other.

Federal scheduling puts organic certification out of reach for cannabis, so the vocabulary had to do the work the word would have: living soil, sun-grown, hand-trimmed. Precise, verifiable, and none of it a claim we couldn't defend.

**The moon system.** New York required effect and potency on every package, but the industry's vocabulary — indica, sativa, hybrid — asks a new consumer to learn a taxonomy before they can buy. The system needed something readable at arm's length that didn't make a claim.

The answer came from the farm. The cultivation team was already working in biodynamics, planting and harvesting against the lunar calendar: full moon for top growth, new moon for roots. The same arc mapped onto the effect scale consumers were being asked to navigate — expansive at full, restorative at new. A moon dial gave the brand an indicator that was instantly legible, connotative without being a promise, and rooted in how the plants were actually grown.

It scaled everywhere: effect badges on flower, vape, and RSO; the sticker system; the icon set; and its own campaign in "Once in a (Super) Blue Moon."
