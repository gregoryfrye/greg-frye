Digital was the bridge — the discipline I came in with, and the one that turned out to be the connective tissue between the farm and the market. Not a campaign calendar: a system built to publish at the speed the harvest moved.

**Voice.** Cannabis marketing runs tired. The work jumped off the expected into something slightly adjacent — terpene wordplay for people who knew what limonene was, a diesel line timed to marathon weekend, effect language built on lunar phase. New York culture and weed culture both reward the same instinct: real ones know. The writing was built for the people who knew, and it flattered them for knowing.

I wrote all of it. Copy wasn't a separate discipline handed off after design; the pun and the layout were conceived together, which is why the campaigns hold across formats.

**Content.** Brand imagery came from the farm on the farm's schedule — harvest happens once a year and doesn't wait for a production calendar. Seasonal shoots with Adam Whyte (product campaigns, farm documentation), Todd Midler (processing, harvest, team interviews), and Meredith Jenks (NYC Diesel drop) built a library covering growing, processing, people, and product. The brand never had to reach for stock or borrow a look it hadn't earned.

What I didn't produce directly got templated, so a small team could work against the system without a designer in the loop for every asset. That was deliberate: my time went to strategy with the CEO, the product roadmap, and helping operations build coordination across departments.
