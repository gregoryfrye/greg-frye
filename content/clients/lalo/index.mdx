<SpineSection label="Context" subhead="A stroller start-up taking on industry giants">

Lalo launched direct-to-consumer into a category dominated by legacy brands, leading with one product: the Daily Stroller.

Rachel Yeager and I met the founders first, to set strategy and direction. I led the design: name, identity, and the store.

</SpineSection>

<SpineSection label="Complication" subhead="Three audiences, one first purchase">

New parents doing the research, and grandparents who'd often pay. A baby gear purchase is high-stakes and heavily researched, and a new brand has no reputation to lean on.

Trust, a smooth path to purchase, and a system that could stretch as the line grew were the launch priorities.

</SpineSection>

<SpineSection label="The Turn" subhead="One week to rename a brand already in production">

The founders had been building under the name Momo, and so had we. Shortly before launch, a trademark conflict ruled it out.

We had one week to find a name that fit the brand world already built, without delaying the first production run of strollers.

</SpineSection>

<SpineSection label="Approach" subhead="Simple, researched, well-made — and answer every question">

A new brand can't buy reputation. It can answer every question first. The brand made one promise: fewer, better baby essentials. The site backed it with answers — a dedicated section for the questions parents ask before they buy, built into navigation alongside the product.

</SpineSection>

## Results

- Featured in GQ — "The stroller start-up taking on industry giants"
- Renamed in a one-week sprint, with no delay to the first production run
